Student Project & Viva Guide: Geo-Fenced Panchnama

1. What is a "Panchnama" in Indian Agriculture & Governance?

A Panchnama (पंचनामा) is a legally recognized on-ground record prepared by revenue officials (such as the Patwari or Village Revenue Officer) in the presence of 5 local village witnesses (Panchas) and the affected farmer. It officially documents natural disaster damage (floods, hailstorms, droughts) to enable compensation under programs like PMFBY (Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana) and the State Disaster Response Fund (SDRF).

2. Key Web Technologies Used (Single File, Pure Vanilla Stack)

  • HTML5 Semantic Elements: <header>, <main>, <section>, <canvas> for accessible, clean structure.
  • W3C Geolocation API: navigator.geolocation.getCurrentPosition() retrieves device latitude and longitude directly in the browser when permission is granted.
  • FileReader API: reader.readAsDataURL() reads the farmer's uploaded image client-side without sending it to an external server.
  • HTML5 Canvas API: ctx.getImageData() demonstrates visual pixel sampling (measuring green healthy pixels vs brown flood/mud pixels).
  • Web Storage API: localStorage preserves saved Panchnamas across browser refreshes with zero backend database.
  • Print CSS Media Query: @media print automatically strips navigation bars and buttons to render a pristine government certificate when printing or saving as PDF.

3. Why Geo-Fencing is Crucial

Historically, insurance fraud occurred when photographs of damaged crops from a completely different region were submitted. Geo-Fencing compares the surveyor's live GPS coordinates against the registered survey number's cadastral boundary. If the surveyor is more than a specified threshold (e.g., 250 meters) away from the farm plot, the system flags a discrepancy.

4. Transparent Demonstration vs Production AI Systems

In this Beginner Project: The damage severity is determined using a transparent rule-based algorithm combined with user-selected damage categories and an educational canvas pixel inspector.

In an Enterprise/Production System: A production deployment would integrate:

  1. A trained Computer Vision model (e.g. YOLOv8, ResNet-50, or Vision Transformers) trained on thousands of labeled drone/mobile crop damage photos.
  2. Satellite Remote Sensing imagery (ISRO Bhuvan / ESA Sentinel-2) to cross-verify Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) before and after the calamity.
  3. Cryptographically signed EXIF metadata to ensure tamper-proof timestamps and location coordinates.
